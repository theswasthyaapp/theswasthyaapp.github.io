Swasthya Privacy Policy

Swasthya, a calm daily companion for body and mind, for iPhone.

In short

Swasthya keeps what you log on your iPhone. There is no account, no server, no advertising, no analytics and no tracking. Anything that leaves the phone goes only where you send it: a backup file you save, a folder you pick, a picture you share, or a feedback email you write.

What stays on your iPhone

Everything you log: workouts and sets, vows, weight and measurements, sleep, meals, notes, the monthly letters and your settings.

Photos you add: Keep's month moments and Darpana's progress photos. Both are left out of the phone's own iCloud backup, and progress photos open only with Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode. The app never puts these photos in your Photos library.

Recordings you make or import, such as mantras, cues and the Om loop.

Deleting the app, or Erase all data in Backup & restore, deletes all of this from the phone.

Apple Health

With your permission, Swasthya reads from Apple Health to show your own progress inside the app: workouts, heart rate, resting heart rate, heart rate variability and VO2 max; active energy, steps, and walking, running, cycling and swimming distance; weight, body fat, waist, height, date of birth and sex; sleep, mindful minutes and time in daylight.

It writes only the weight and waist measurements you enter in Swasthya. Health data is never used for advertising, never shared, and never leaves the phone through the app except inside a backup you set up. Data written to Health stays there after the app is deleted; you manage it in the Health app.

What the app asks for, and why

Location: your approximate location while the app is open, to work out sunrise, sunset and the panchangam. Kept only on the phone.

Camera: to take a progress photo, or a photo of a workout sheet you want read into a log.

Microphone: to record mantras and cues in your own voice.

Photos: Apple's photo picker hands the app only the photos you choose. Save Image in a share asks once to add that picture to your library; the app can't see the library.

Face ID or passcode: to open Darpana's progress photos.

Notifications and alarms: reminders, the weekly summary, the morning nudge and wake-up alarms, all scheduled on the phone. No push server is involved.

Siri and Shortcuts: Log my weight, Keep my vow and How's my week answer with your weight, a vow's name or your week, only when you ask, and on a locked iPhone they ask for Face ID first. What Siri hears and says is handled by Apple, under Apple's privacy policy.

A workout sheet is read on the iPhone with Apple's on-device text recognition and, where available, Apple's on-device language model. The photo is not uploaded.

Backups and sharing, by your choice

Export saves a backup file wherever you choose in Files.

Automatic backup, once you pick a folder, writes a daily copy there, with Keep's photos and your recordings beside it. Progress photos join only if you turn that on in Darpana.

If the folder you pick is in iCloud Drive, Apple stores those files under your Apple Account. The developer has no access to them.

Sharing a week card or a milestone picture happens only through the share sheet you open, to the place you pick.

Backup files stay where you saved them until you delete them, including after the app is deleted.

What the developer receives

Only what you send. Swasthya contains no third-party code for analytics, advertising or crash reporting, and it makes no network requests of its own.

Send feedback (Settings) opens your email app with a message to theswasthyaapp@gmail.com, with the app's version, the iOS version and the iPhone model, and, if you leave it on, how many times you have opened each part of the app, counted only on your iPhone. Nothing is sent until you press Send, and you can change or delete any of it first. Feedback is used only to reply and to improve the app.

If you allow it in iOS Settings, Apple may share crash reports and usage statistics with app developers. That is Apple's feature, covered by Apple's privacy policy.

During beta testing, TestFlight passes on the feedback and screenshots you choose to send, crash reports, and basic install and session details, as Apple describes in TestFlight.

Children

Swasthya is not designed for children under 13. The app itself collects no personal information from anyone.
