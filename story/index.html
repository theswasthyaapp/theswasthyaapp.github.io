स्वास्थ्य

The story behind Swasthya

Svāsthya: health. Word by word, being established in yourself: sva, the self, and stha, to stand.

Why I started

Early this year I decided to take my health seriously. I wanted to lose weight, get fit, feel strong, and stay motivated enough not to give up until I reached my goal.

So I changed my days: a personal trainer twice a week before work, the treadmill on the mornings in between, yoga when I had the energy, and early nights. And I gave up some bad habits.

Why I built an app

I work in tech, and I like to track everything. At first I logged the lifts from my trainer’s sessions in a simple web page, so I could repeat them on the other days. It worked, but my day holds more than sets and reps: a morning routine, the promises I make to myself, a few minutes of breathing, sleep, what’s on my plate, and the festivals I grew up with.

Then my trainer’s schedule and mine stopped lining up, and I didn’t want to stop what I’d started. So the app had to plan my training when my trainer couldn’t: a plan for the week, and a nudge when I’m ready to lift more.

No single app held all of that. The ones I tried were busy, full of ads, or wanted an account and my data. So in the fall I opened Xcode for the first time and started building my own iPhone app, one feature at a time. Claude, an AI assistant, was my pair programmer the whole way.

How I use it every day

Dina
Each morning starts here: my own alarm, a greeting, the day’s vows and what’s next.
Vyayama
I check my plan, turn on focus mode in the gym, and look up a move’s form when I need to. My Apple Watch fills in the treadmill and my heart rate.
Yoga, Dhyana
Yoga on the lighter days, and a few quiet minutes of breath.
Sankalpa
My vows. Each cycle I keep puts a flower or a fruit in its basket.
Nidra
It wakes me gently and keeps my early nights honest.
Each week
On Sundays I look back at my week, and each month ends with a letter about what went well.

What’s inside

Each part of Swasthya carries a Sanskrit name, and the app explains what it means. Here is the whole tour, so you don’t miss anything.

  1. दिन

    Dina the day

    Your morning in one place: a greeting, a verse from the Bhagavad Gita, your vows and what’s next. Answer a few questions and it plans your Dinacharya, the day’s routine: training, cardio, steps, meals and sleep.

  2. पञ्चाङ्ग

    Panchangam the calendar

    Each day’s tithi and nakshatra, the good times to begin and the times to avoid, worked out on your iPhone for where you are. Festivals appear on their day, each with its story, where it comes from in the scriptures, and what it means.

  3. व्यायाम

    Vyayama exercise

    Your training week, planned, and a nudge when you’re ready to lift more. Focus mode for the gym, 135 moves with pictures and form cues, and your Apple Watch’s workouts and heart rate, with Zone 2 for cardio.

  4. चक्र

    The seven chakras the inner fire

    On the week card, an inner fire climbs the seven chakras as your active days add up. Meet the week’s goal and the crown blooms with your chosen deity’s blessing. A page explains each chakra: where it sits, its element and its seed sound.

  5. योग

    Yoga union

    Eight guided practices, from Surya Namaskar with the sun’s twelve names to Chandra Namaskara. Its painting follows your week: the sun rises over the river as your yoga days add up. And the story of yoga, from the Rig Veda to today.

  6. ध्यान

    Dhyana meditation

    Seven breathing practices, from Nadi Shodhana to Bhramari, and an Om meditation. Its painting follows you too: the clouds part over the mountain with your days of stillness, and 7, 21, 40 and 108 days each have their own moment.

  7. संकल्प

    Sankalpa resolve

    Your vows, kept one cycle at a time. Each cycle you keep puts a flower or a fruit in its basket, and your garden of resolves grows.

  8. आहार

    Ahara food

    Meal times and a protein goal from your plan, ideas for the next plate, and a plate maker to check a meal before you eat it.

  9. निद्रा

    Nidra sleep

    Swasthya’s own wake-up alarm, which starts soft. Log last night with one tap from the Lock Screen, and see how steady your lights-out has been.

  10. स्थिति

    The climb and Sthiti keeping it

    Your weight goal is a mountain, with camps on the way up. The summit isn’t the end: Keep helps you stay in a range around your goal, with a lamp you light at each weigh-in, a lotus for each month in range, and a next summit that isn’t about the scale.

  11. स्मरण · दर्पण

    Smarana and Darpana remembering, the mirror

    A look back every Sunday, and a letter at the end of each month about what went well. Darpana keeps your progress photos behind Face ID, never in your photo library.

  12. Widgets and Siri at a glance

    Your week, your vows and your climb on the Home and Lock Screens. Ask Siri to log your weight, keep a vow, start a workout or start Dhyana.

What changed

I’m lighter and stronger, and my days have a rhythm. Most of all, I’ve learned to keep small promises to myself, one day at a time.

Giving up some habits was hard, so I built a busy life outside work to keep myself motivated: new DIY projects, and meaningful ways to spend my time. This app is one of them, and more will come, depending on the response.

Why I’m sharing it

I want to share my journey. What worked for me might work for someone else too, so the app is yours, free. Everything you log stays on your iPhone: no account, no ads, no tracking.

It’s rooted in the traditions I grew up with, from the Gita’s verses to the stories behind each festival.

If it helps you keep one promise to yourself, it has done its job. Tell me what works and what doesn’t: Send feedback in Settings, or write to theswasthyaapp@gmail.com.
